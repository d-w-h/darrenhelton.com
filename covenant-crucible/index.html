Covenant & Crucible

What changed in US trade policy, as it was published

Covenant & Crucible read US trade policy as it was published and told importers, customs brokers and compliance teams what had changed. It watched more than thirty government and trade-data sources: the Federal Register, Customs and Border Protection, the US Trade Representative, the Commerce Department's list of restricted parties, the World Trade Organization and others.

AI read every new document and scored how much it mattered, so the changes that counted reached people first. The scores became alerts, the total tariff on a product by its customs code, and measures such as how fast the rules were changing, which goods were at risk of being reclassified, and where shipments were piling up. A screening service checked names against 223,000 people and companies on government sanctions and watch lists.

It shipped as a web portal, an API for other software to call, and a daily email digest.

Forecast

The alerts were the first layer. The product was forecast: twelve models, each one trainable from records the government already publishes, so the system could say not just what had changed but what was coming. Among them: a quiet spell in routine trade notices as the sign that a major action is days away; how much a product's imports fall for each point of tariff; where trade moves when a tariff lands on one country; the rush of shipments before a tariff takes effect; and when, and on which goods, a trading partner hits back.

The ingestion was built for it: every field kept, the full text kept, every timestamp kept, so the models would have history to learn from.
